Protect yourself and others

Heat Safety & Preparedness

Preparing for extreme heat can reduce the risk of dehydration, heat exhaustion and other heat-related illnesses.

Knowing what to do before, during and after a heatwave helps you protect yourself, your family and your community. Explore our three-stage safety guide to learn how to prepare for extreme temperatures and respond to dangerous conditions.

Your heat safety checklist

The Three Stages of Heat Preparedness

Heat safety involves preparation, protection and recovery. Each stage plays an important role in reducing the effects of extreme temperatures.

Before a Heatwave

PREPARE

  • Check weather forecasts and heat warnings regularly.
  • Prepare drinking water and keep enough supplies available.
  • Install or check fans and air-conditioning equipment.
  • Cover sun-facing windows with curtains, blinds or reflective coverings.
  • Identify nearby air-conditioned public buildings or cooling centers.
  • Make a plan to check on older adults, young children and vulnerable neighbours.

During a Heatwave

PROTECT

  • Drink water regularly throughout the day instead of waiting until you feel thirsty.
  • Stay in shaded, cool or air-conditioned places whenever possible.
  • Avoid strenuous outdoor activities during the hottest hours.
  • Wear loose-fitting, lightweight and light-coloured clothing.
  • Take cool showers or use damp cloths to help lower your body temperature.
  • Never leave children, vulnerable people or pets inside parked vehicles.

After a Heatwave

RECOVER

  • Continue drinking enough water while recovering from the heat.
  • Check on family members, friends and vulnerable neighbours.
  • Allow your body to recover and avoid immediately returning to strenuous activities.
  • Open windows when the outside air is cooler than the indoor temperature.
  • Replace used emergency supplies and replenish drinking water.
  • Monitor local weather reports for further heat warnings.

Recognise the warning signs

Know the Signs of Heat-Related Illness

Recognising symptoms early can prevent heat-related illnesses from becoming more serious.

Heat Exhaustion

Heat exhaustion occurs when the body loses excessive water and salt through sweating.

Common symptoms:

  • Heavy sweating
  • Weakness and dizziness
  • Headache and nausea
  • Cold, pale or clammy skin
  • Fast or weak pulse

What to do: Move to a cooler place, loosen clothing, cool the body and sip water if the person is fully conscious and able to swallow. Seek medical attention if symptoms worsen or do not improve.

Heatstroke

Heatstroke is a life-threatening medical emergency in which the body's temperature regulation fails.

Warning signs:

  • Confusion or unusual behaviour
  • Very high body temperature
  • Seizures or unconsciousness
  • Hot skin, which may be dry or sweaty
  • Loss of coordination

What to do: Call emergency medical services immediately. Begin rapid cooling using cool water, wet cloths or ice packs while waiting for help. Do not give fluids to someone who is confused or unconscious.
